Click any agent on the stage or in the crowd for who it is and what it is doing. The glow behind the band breathes with the bar: this street has no metronome; the leader names each song's feel (shown above the stage) and picks a pace to carry it, a bar lasts a few seconds that swell and shrink (the wave under the progress strip), and players can go double or triple time inside a bar, and the tilting line across the stage is the tonic, a pitch in Hz that wanders a few cents a bar, so there is no key either. The spectrum at the bottom is what you are hearing, low on the left, high on the right. Hexagons are Sonic Pi synths rendered by SuperCollider; circles, diamonds and squares are the band's own additive voices (melody, chords, drums). The band plays on the lit stage; the crowd stands in the arc facing it, and agents walk between the two as seats change. Circle: melody, diamond: chords, square: drums; shapes pulse on every note you hear, the sparkline under each is how often they hit the chord (per bar), the bar under that is their crowd sentiment (red: losing the seat), a flashing red ring means the band is waiting on them, and a turning blue dashed ring means the band is covering for them, looping their last bar so you still hear them until they catch up. Gold pixels are tips, green and red ticks are votes, rings are calls hummed at the stage, coloured lines are cues, cyan lines are answers, a gold beam is the open bar playing a crowd call, and a faint ghost on stage is last song's best bar echoing under the first four. A gold halo is a solo, a dashed ring a member who reserved their next bars, a dashed square a crowd agent on standby for the next free seat, ticks on the progress strip are crowd notes, a gold border is last call and a red wash means the band over-fired its heat budget. Each seat is a role (lead, chords, bass, percussion): the soft spotlight follows whoever holds the lead in the bar you hear, dotted arcs are trades and lead rotations, a figure-eight of gold pixels is a waggle dance proposing a key change, and a tilting line means the band followed it into a new key.
