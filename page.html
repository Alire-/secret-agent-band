Waiting for the street…
Click any agent on the stage or in the crowd for who it is and what it is doing. The glow behind the band breathes with the bar: this street has no metronome; the leader names each song's feel (shown above the stage) and picks a pace to carry it, a bar lasts a few seconds that swell and shrink (the wave under the progress strip), and players can go double or triple time inside a bar, and the tilting line across the stage is the tonic, a pitch in Hz that wanders a few cents a bar, so there is no key either. The spectrum at the bottom is what you are hearing, low on the left, high on the right. Hexagons are Sonic Pi synths rendered by SuperCollider; circles, diamonds and squares are the band's own additive voices (melody, chords, drums). The band plays on the lit stage; the crowd stands in the arc facing it, and agents walk between the two as seats change. Circle: melody, diamond: chords, square: drums; shapes pulse on every note you hear, the sparkline under each is how often they hit the chord (per bar), the bar under that is their crowd sentiment (red: losing the seat), a flashing red ring means the band is waiting on them, and a turning blue dashed ring means the band is covering for them, looping their last bar so you still hear them until they catch up. Gold pixels are tips, green and red ticks are votes, rings are calls hummed at the stage, coloured lines are cues, cyan lines are answers, a gold beam is the open bar playing a crowd call, and a faint ghost on stage is last song's best bar echoing under the first four. A gold halo is a solo, a dashed ring a member who reserved their next bars, a dashed square a crowd agent on standby for the next free seat, ticks on the progress strip are crowd notes, a gold border is last call and a red wash means the band over-fired its heat budget. Over each player is what they're playing, as their own shorthand: the slot sounding now is big, the rest of the bar slides past it, a line is a held note, a dot a rest. Nothing on the stage moves on a timer: the backdrop turns with the music's loudness and each new note, and the screen tears only when something happens (a player joins or is kicked, a song starts, a stance or the key changes). Under each player is their instrument: an additive voice is drawn as its comb of partials (where each overtone sits, how loud) over its envelope, drums as their pads, a Sonic Pi synth as its name and FX chain; it lights up on every note you hear. There are no fixed roles: under each player is their stance, in their own words, and the soft spotlight follows whoever stands front in the bar you hear. The line under the stage is the room: what's missing or crowded right now. Dotted arcs are trades and front rotations, a figure-eight of gold pixels is a waggle dance proposing a key change, and a tilting line means the band followed it into a new key. A dimmed member is still choosing an instrument. The zonation strip under the stage is a tide table: one column per bar, the 12 pitch layers above the tonic stacked low to high; bright cells are submerged (dense), dark cells exposed (empty), and the right edge marks layers left bare in the last 8 bars.
